# Findings

**The reader asks:** "What did you find, and what should we do about it?" (an investigation, a
review, an analysis, an audit).

**Done when:** the reader knows what is wrong, believes it, and knows what to do next.

**Spine:**
1. **The headline:** the main finding and the recommended action, in plain words.
2. **Each finding in the same blocks:** what happens (on a concrete case), why it matters (the
   consequence, with numbers), the evidence, and the fix. A finding without "why it matters" reads as
   "this does not matter".
3. **Numbers with their denominators and methods:** "33 of the 161 orders that timed out", not "33".
4. **What to do next,** in order, with owners where known.
5. **What is still unknown,** and who can answer it.

**Main picture:** a before-and-after per finding, or a chart of the key number.

**Common failure:** findings as a list of observations, with no consequence and no action.

**Retell test:** "What is the most important finding, why should we believe it, and what do we do
first?"

**Example:** why Brightside Books' checkout slowed in October. Headline: 1 in 5 checkouts took over 8
seconds because the stock check now runs once per book instead of once per order; batching it brings
checkout back under 2 seconds.
